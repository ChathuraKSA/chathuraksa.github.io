Research

PhD · Queen Mary University of London

The adoption to actionability gap

My PhD asked why educators adopt learning analytics far more often than they act on them. It produced a three-layer conceptual framework, four learner archetypes and a validated toolkit.

Toolkit · LAK 2025

A planner educators can use

I built the Learning Activity Planner to map analytics markers to teaching decisions and learning design activity types, so educators can act on what the data shows.

Now · GenAI in assessment

When AI can simulate learning

I now ask how we evaluate competency when AI can simulate learning. The framework is tested in a first-year module and published in the LAK 2026 workshop proceedings.

The approach

Equip, Evidence, Evaluate.

Detecting AI use is an arms race. Making thinking visible is a design choice. Three moves turn that choice into assessment.

E1

Equip

Prepare students to use GenAI critically before they are assessed. Weekly worksheets build the habit, starting with structured prompting.

E2

Evidence

Make the process visible with an AI-use log, staged checkpoints, and a contribution record or live verification for group work.

E3

Evaluate

Judge competency from the process as well as the product, using criteria that stay manageable for markers.
